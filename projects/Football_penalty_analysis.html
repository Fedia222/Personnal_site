Statistical Modelling of Football Penalty Shootouts

A university project exploring historical World Cup penalty shootouts through statistics and probability, with the aim of understanding shot patterns and modelling strategies that maximise the probability of scoring or saving a penalty.

University Project Statistics & Probability In Progress

Context

I started this project as part of my university studies to apply statistical and probabilistic methods to a concrete decision-making problem: football penalty shootouts. Historical data provides a way to explore where players shoot, how goalkeepers react and how these choices relate to the outcome.

Beyond describing past penalties, the project asks how these observations could inform the next decision. Given a model of the goalkeeper’s behaviour and the shooter’s chances of success in different zones, where should the next penalty be aimed?

Exploratory Analysis

  • Data preparation: Organising historical World Cup shootout data, including shot zones, outcomes and kick sequence, into a format suitable for statistical analysis.
  • Descriptive statistics: Examining shot frequencies and observed scoring rates across different areas of the goal.
  • Visual exploration: Using distribution plots and spatial visualisations to investigate shooting patterns and goalkeeper behaviour.
  • Comparisons: Exploring relationships between shot placement, goalkeeper decisions and penalty outcomes, where the available data supports these comparisons.

Probabilistic Modelling

The modelling direction is to represent shooting zones and goalkeeper choices as discrete random variables, and the outcome of a penalty as a binary variable: goal or no goal. This provides a framework for moving from observed frequencies to estimated probability distributions.

  • Discrete distributions: Model the probabilities of shooting towards different zones and of the goalkeeper choosing different directions.
  • Conditional probabilities: Estimate the probability of scoring given the shot zone and, when recorded, the goalkeeper’s response.
  • Bernoulli and binomial models: Use a Bernoulli model for an individual penalty outcome and consider a binomial model for repeated comparable attempts when independence and a common success probability are reasonable assumptions.
  • Statistical uncertainty: Assess how sample size and variability affect the reliability of estimated scoring probabilities, particularly for rarely targeted zones.

From Probabilities to Strategy

A central objective is to compare possible shot placements by their estimated probability of success. For each zone, the model would combine the possible goalkeeper responses with the probability of scoring against each response. The zone with the highest resulting probability would be the preferred choice under that model.

The same reasoning can be explored from the goalkeeper’s perspective: how should a goalkeeper choose a direction given the distribution of the opponent’s shots? A further extension is to study mixed strategies, where choices are randomised to avoid becoming predictable.

Any recommendation depends on the model assumptions and available information. An apparent advantage in historical data may not generalise to a particular player, goalkeeper or match situation, especially if opponents adapt their behaviour.

Current Progress & Next Steps

The work so far focuses on data exploration, descriptive statistics and initial visualisations. These analyses provide the basis for developing and evaluating the probabilistic models.

  • Formalise the probability distributions and estimate conditional scoring probabilities.
  • Quantify uncertainty and examine whether observed differences are supported by enough data.
  • Compare model-based strategies with simple baseline choices and evaluate them on held-out data where feasible.
  • Explore whether kick sequence or previous attempts add useful information, rather than assuming that successive penalties are dependent.

What I Am Learning

This project connects exploratory data analysis, probability distributions, statistical estimation and decision-making under uncertainty. It also highlights the importance of distinguishing an observed pattern from a reliable prediction, and a model-based strategy from one demonstrated to work in practice.
